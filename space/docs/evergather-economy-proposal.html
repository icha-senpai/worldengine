Progression and decisions

Agreed progression

Ten equipment and production tiers at levels 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, and 100. Introductory level 1–9 gathering collects the same tier-one raw materials. Tier-one production and improved equipment unlock at 10. Each skill has ten primary outputs, one per tier. Actions can be condensed or expanded.

Starter training

All 38 skills receive an introductory action available below skill level 10. Training grants XP and a small appropriate tier-one raw-material reward, never a tier-one processed component or upgraded tool. Examples: Smelting sorts Copper Ore; Weaving prepares Hemp Fiber; Carpentry sorts Ash Logs. Training is replaced by normal tiered work at level 10. All skills receive Basic equipment at character creation. Starter repair costs gold and requires no processed material.

Action layout

Seven gathering skills have ten material targets each: 70 gathering targets. Twenty-one primary skill lines produce their outputs by consuming materials: processing, crafting, Survival, Cartography, Leadership, and Trading. Nine non-production activity lines have ten encounters/routes each: 90 activities. Reputation has ten guild commissions that consume Supply Crates. Crafting and processing use the recipe interface instead of a parallel free-loot activity. Add 38 clearly separated introductory training actions. Expand secondary actions only when they provide a distinct material route or meaningful play choice.

Naming and roles

Names identify the actual material or resource: Copper Ore, Bronze Ingot, Ash Handle, Hemp Binding, and Quartz Runestone. Early tiers use familiar materials; later tiers introduce fantasy materials. There are no numbered Common Tooling rewards. Regions name maps, permits, medical notes, and orders because those resources refer to a destination or institution. Mining supplies ore; Excavation is deliberately refocused on buried gemstones. Farming supplies fibers; Fishing supplies the first food line. Additional crop, meat, stone, or ceramic lines should be added only as full ten-tier families with real consumers.

Tier and rarity

Item tier is independent of rarity. Six rarity qualities remain metadata on the same item identity. Proposed caps: T1 Common, T2 Uncommon, T3 Rare, T4 Epic, T5 Legendary, T6–T10 Mythic. A rarity attempt consumes same-tier Extract, Runestone, Relic Core, and Guild Permit plus gold. Preserve the current progress-on-failure system; chance, quantities, prices, and tier caps in this draft still need balancing.

Equipment gates

A crafter needs the assembling profession at the item tier gate. Equipping requires the destination skill at that gate too, including purchased or traded tools. Crafting never requires the new tool it is trying to create. Starter tools remain usable through the first upgrade. Recipes consume component bundles; finished equipment is never an intermediate ingredient.

Repairs upgrades and salvage

Repair consumes a tool’s first listed assembly component and gold; scale material count to missing durability. Tier upgrades consume the next-tier component bundle and gold, update the existing tool, and preserve its rarity within the new cap. Salvage returns less material than crafting consumed and cannot be a profitable item-generation loop. Starter repair remains gold-only.

Consumables

Rations are usable XP buffs, tonics are usable yield buffs, and arrow bundles are optional Ranged bonuses. Use ten charges rather than timers so they work well in a click-based game. One active buff per family; show remaining charges, replacements, and effective bonuses clearly. Exact percentages are undecided. Bandages are real Healing activity supplies, rather than promising an HP system this game does not currently have. Oils feed varnish recipes.

Jobs and expeditions

Jobs consume new resources through delivery, medical, coastal, and survey contracts. Expeditions consume same-tier Crew Orders and Rations and return useful existing materials from their destination’s tier, never bespoke trophy items. Reputation commissions consume Supply Crates and grant Guild Permits plus Reputation XP. Delivery contracts consume Supply Crates for gold and Trading XP. Tune outputs and gold so crafting, delivery, purchasing, and salvage cannot form a guaranteed gold loop.

Scope and replacement

This is an item-economy proposal for the separate Space Evergather game. Keep the 38 skills, character identity, skill XP, and gold; replace item definitions, recipes, tool definitions, starter gear, action loot, jobs, expeditions, shop offers, and item-linked achievements together. Reset legacy item stacks and tools, replace starters, cancel obsolete contracts and listings, and reconcile any currency already held in pending trades. Rebuild item catalog projections and material-source guidance. No housing, settlement, world-engine, or simulated naval-combat subsystem is included. The live game has not been changed.

Verification before reset

Back up the current data and produce exact affected-row counts before applying the replacement. Validate every item has a source and sink, every input exists, no recipe needs a higher-tier input, every tier is reachable from introductory actions, and every equipment slot has ten upgrades. Verify shop/market transfer gates, buff charge consumption, jobs, repairs, rarity attempts, salvage, and the materials popup against the new catalog. The checks performed for this proposal cover identity, coverage, sources, sinks, and graph reachability; they are not runtime or economy-balance tests.

Discussion status

The tier gates, first-resource access at levels 1–9, starter gear, and permission to reshape actions are agreed. Resource names, recipes, the Excavation/Farming/Furniture role choices, buff mechanics, rarity caps, and the reset handling below are proposed for review. Quantity values are placeholders for balancing. No runtime catalog or database mutation is made by generating this proposal.
